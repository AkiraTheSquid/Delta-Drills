## Concept

Mastery is not permanent. Even a concept you answered correctly ten times
in a row fades from memory if you never see it again — the estimate the app
holds for you decays a little with every day that passes without practice,
faster right after you learned it and more slowly the longer it has stuck.
This is the forgetting curve: memory strength rises sharply from practice
and drops off over time, and the drop is steeper the newer the memory is.

Spaced repetition is the app's answer to that curve: instead of drilling a
concept once and moving on for good, it schedules a review to land close to
the point where you would otherwise have forgotten it — late enough that
recalling it takes real effort (which is what strengthens it further), but
before the estimate has fallen so far that the concept counts as unlearned
again. A concept you have seen many times, spread out, gets a longer gap
before its next review than one you just barely learned.

## Worked example

You learn a concept on Monday. Its forgetting curve says that by Wednesday,
without another look, your recall probability would drop below the learned
threshold. The app schedules a review for Wednesday morning instead of, say,
next Monday — early enough to catch you before you have actually forgotten
it, which is also the point where a review does the most good.

You get the Wednesday review right. That correct, effortful recall pushes
the next natural forgetting point further out — maybe a week and a half away
this time — so the app schedules the following review for then, not for the
next day. Each successful spaced review buys a longer gap before the next
one is needed.

three times since, spaced out; the other has not been touched since the day
it was learned. Which one's mastery estimate has likely decayed further by
today?
